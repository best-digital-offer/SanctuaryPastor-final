New Testament Books in Order: A Simple Guide for Reading and Prayer

See the 27 New Testament books in canonical order, understand the main groups, and use practical reading and prayer prompts to study them.

The New Testament contains 27 books, beginning with Matthew and ending with Revelation in the canonical order followed by most English-language Bibles. Knowing the sequence helps you navigate Scripture and choose a starting point for study and prayer.

The 27 New Testament books in order

  1. Matthew
  2. Mark
  3. Luke
  4. John
  5. Acts
  6. Romans
  7. 1 Corinthians
  8. 2 Corinthians
  9. Galatians
  10. Ephesians
  11. Philippians
  12. Colossians
  13. 1 Thessalonians
  14. 2 Thessalonians
  15. 1 Timothy
  16. 2 Timothy
  17. Titus
  18. Philemon
  19. Hebrews
  20. James
  21. 1 Peter
  22. 2 Peter
  23. 1 John
  24. 2 John
  25. 3 John
  26. Jude
  27. Revelation

This is the usual 27-book New Testament sequence. Bible canons and the arrangement of Old Testament books differ among Christian traditions; for an authoritative reference, consult a Bible edition used by your church or a recognised publisher such as Bible Gateway's guide to the books of the Bible.

How the books are grouped

The four Gospels—Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John present accounts of Jesus' life, teaching, death, and resurrection from distinctive perspectives. Reading them slowly is a useful way to begin if you want to focus on Jesus' ministry.

Acts tells of the early Christian movement and its spread. The letters address faith, community, worship, conduct, and specific questions faced by early churches and individuals. Revelation uses vivid apocalyptic imagery and pastoral encouragement; it benefits from careful attention to its literary form and historical setting.

Where should a beginner start?

If you are new to the New Testament, you could read one Gospel first, then Acts, and then choose a short letter such as Philippians or James. This is a suggested learning path, not a rule. Some people prefer to follow a church reading plan or move through the books in printed order.

Set a small goal—perhaps one paragraph or one chapter—and ask what the passage says before deciding what it means for you. Avoid rushing through difficult passages just to complete a checklist.

A reading-and-prayer method

  1. Read the passage in context. Include the verses before and after the section.
  2. Summarise the main idea. Write one sentence in your own words.
  3. Ask a question. Note what is clear and what needs further study.
  4. Respond in prayer. Give thanks, confess a needed change, ask for wisdom, or pray for someone affected by the passage.
  5. Choose one action. Decide whether there is a practical next step you can take.

A Bible study journal can help track references and questions without requiring long notes every day.

Use the order as a guide, not a finish line

Some books are letters meant to be read as a whole; others contain poetry, visions, or narrative. Their canonical sequence is not always a timeline of when they were written. When a passage is confusing, consult a study Bible, a trusted commentary, or a knowledgeable church leader rather than forcing a quick answer.

For more structured reflection, explore the SOAP Bible study method and the Bible study journal guide. A good routine makes space for careful reading, prayer, and questions.

Frequently asked questions

How many books are in the New Testament?

There are 27 books in the New Testament in the standard Christian Bible canon.

Are the New Testament books listed in chronological order?

No. The usual printed order is canonical, not a precise writing timeline. Some letters were likely written before the Gospels, and chronology is more complex than the table of contents suggests.
