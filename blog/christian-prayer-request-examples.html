Christian Prayer Request: Examples and How to Share One Thoughtfully

Learn how to write a clear Christian prayer request, see examples for common situations, and protect privacy when sharing requests with others.

A prayer request helps other people understand what you are asking them to bring before God. It does not need to include every detail. A clear, respectful request tells others what kind of support would be helpful while protecting personal information.

How to write a clear prayer request

Start with the main concern, then say what kind of prayer or support would be meaningful. You might ask for wisdom during a decision, strength through a difficult season, comfort after a loss, or encouragement for a family member. Include only details the people involved have agreed to share.

Short requests can be easier for a group to remember and revisit. You can always speak privately with a trusted pastor, chaplain, or friend when a situation needs more context than a public request should contain.

Prayer request examples you can adapt

For guidance: “Please pray that I have wisdom and peace as I consider an important work decision, and that I listen carefully to good advice.”

For a family member who is ill: “Please pray for comfort, strength, and good care for someone in my family during treatment. We would appreciate privacy while we navigate the next steps.”

For grief: “Please pray for our family as we mourn a loss. We are grateful for support, patience, and practical help over the coming weeks.”

For a difficult relationship: “Please pray for wisdom, truth, and healthy boundaries as I work through a challenging relationship.”

For gratitude: “I am thankful for a new opportunity and would appreciate prayer that I use it responsibly and generously.”

What makes a request helpful?

A helpful prayer request is specific enough to guide prayer but not so detailed that it exposes someone else's private life. Ask yourself: Does this information belong to me to share? Would the person be comfortable if this were repeated? Is the request written with dignity rather than gossip or blame?

It is also useful to distinguish a request for prayer from a request for practical assistance. If you need meals, transport, childcare, or someone to attend an appointment with you, say so plainly. People may not realise what would help unless you name it.

Privacy and safety when sharing prayer requests

Avoid posting full names, addresses, medical records, school names, or details of a legal or family crisis in public groups. Share sensitive information only with people who need it and have permission to receive it. If a concern involves abuse, exploitation, self-harm, or immediate danger, seek appropriate professional or emergency support; a prayer request alone is not a safety plan.

Church groups can set simple guidelines: ask permission before sharing another person's story, do not forward private messages without consent, and confirm before repeatedly circulating old requests. Compassion includes protecting dignity.

How to follow up after asking for prayer

If you are comfortable, send a brief update when circumstances change. You can thank people for their support without feeling obliged to share every detail. Some requests remain private or ongoing; it is fine to say that you are still waiting, still grieving, or not ready to discuss it.

Prayer is one part of care. Listening, presence, meals, practical assistance, and referrals to qualified support can also be expressions of compassion. Keep the focus on the person rather than on producing a dramatic outcome.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a prayer request be?

A few sentences are often enough: name the concern, ask for the type of prayer or support you need, and include only details you have permission to share.

Can I submit a prayer request anonymously?

Many groups allow anonymous requests, but practices vary. Ask the group leader how privacy is handled before sharing sensitive information.
