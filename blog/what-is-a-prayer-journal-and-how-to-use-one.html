What Is a Prayer Journal and How Do You Use One?

A practical guide to prayer journals, what to write in one, and how journaling can help organize Scripture, prayer requests, gratitude, and reflection.

What is a prayer journal?

A prayer journal is a notebook or guided journal used to record prayers, Scripture reflections, requests, gratitude, and spiritual observations. Some people write complete prayers, while others use short notes or prompts. There is no single required format. The most useful journal is one that helps you slow down and engage intentionally with prayer.

What can you write in a prayer journal?

You can record people you are praying for, personal concerns, Scripture passages, things you are thankful for, questions you are bringing to God, and ways you have seen prayers answered. You can also write a short prayer after reading a Bible passage. Over time, these entries can become a record of your spiritual reflections.

Try a simple page format

Divide a page into four sections: Scripture, reflection, prayer, and gratitude. Write one verse or passage, note what stands out, respond honestly in prayer, and finish with one thing you are thankful for. This structure works well for a short morning or evening quiet time.

Use a guided journal when you need prompts

A guided prayer journal can be useful when you want more direction. Prompt-based pages reduce the pressure of deciding what to write and can encourage consistency. The collection includes prayer journals, devotionals, ACTS and SOAP-style journals, and topic-focused resources.

Keep the practice personal

A prayer journal is not a performance. Do not worry about writing polished sentences or filling a certain number of pages. Honest notes, short prayers, and simple Scripture reflections can be meaningful. The purpose is to support your prayer life, not to create another task you have to complete perfectly.
